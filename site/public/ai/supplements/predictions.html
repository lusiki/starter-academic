The predictions scoreboard

Every forecast claim, dated, measurable, and gradeable

Supplement
Author

Luka Šikić

Published

July 23, 2026

This is the scoreboard, and it is the only page that changes after the document is tagged v1.0. The forecast prose freezes at release. The retrospective charts keep extending themselves as the corpus grows, and this table gains a resolution (a date, a verdict, and an evidence link) as each claim comes due.

Every prediction obeys the same rules. It is dated. It is binary or numerically measurable. It carries a probability in steps of 0.05. It names how it will be scored, whether from public pages or from this database directly. There are no hedged verbs anywhere in the set, and a claim that cannot be marked true or false does not belong here.

How scoring works

The cheapest claims to grade are the ones this database answers by itself. When the corpus is rebuilt each quarter, build_metrics.R recomputes the numbers that three of these predictions turn on. Those three are the verification-tool share (P1), the Coding-with-AI entry share (P9), and the embargo-resolution rate (P10), so scoring them is a script run, not a judgment call. The rest resolve from public pages. These are journal and data-editor policies, editor reports, job postings, syllabi, and citation searches, each named in the scoring column of predictions.csv.

Code 
kable(
  p |> transmute(ID = id, `How it will be scored` = scoring),
  align = "ll")

P4 Preprint and journal search. This is the shared reporting-standard definition reused by P11
P5 AEA Data Editor and top-five journal policy pages
P6 A quotable public editor statement or annual data-editor note
P7 RA and predoc postings, archived with URL and date at capture (postings are not retrievable later)
P8 rOpenSci, JOSS, and CRAN registries plus corpus tool rows
P9 public product changelogs (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, and the like) plus a hand-labelled sample of new tools
P10 the NBER working-paper ids embargoed at v1.0, scored by a stated procedure over the world (author site, NBER open-chapter series, institutional WP series), not this corpus’s fetch lane
P11 Citation search in peer-reviewed methods sections, using the standard defined in P4
P12 Repository search plus a peer-reviewed citation check, excluding this site’s own tool-labels set
P13 Publicly archived graduate syllabi from distinct institutions
The probabilities are the maintainer’s to set

These probabilities are a first pass and are meant to be reviewed before the v1.0 freeze. Once tagged, they do not change, because a forecast you can revise after the fact is not a forecast. Freezing also pins the definitions. The verification keyword classifier (VERIF_RE), the 2026 H1 baseline P9 measures against (12.4%), the embargoed-id list P10 tracks, and the shared reporting-standard definition P4 and P11 both use are all fixed at the tag, so a prediction cannot be scored against a moved goalpost. The document is designed to be graded in 2028 and again in 2031, and being wrong on the record is the point of writing them down.
