How this was built, and what it cannot verify

Honesty is the product

Supplement
Author

Luka Šikić

Published

July 23, 2026

This document has two kinds of claim and no third kind. Claims about the past are recomputed from a database. Claims about the future carry a probability. This page describes the machinery behind the first kind and states plainly what it cannot support.

The data seam

One script, scenario/R/build_metrics.R, reads data/aiecon.sqlite (SELECT-only, it never writes the database) and emits tidy CSVs into scenario/data/derived/. The .qmd pages read only those CSVs, never the database. Renders stay fast, continuous integration needs no database logic, and a CSV diff makes every data change visible in git. Every number in the prose resolves to one of these files through the accessors in scenario/R/fig_helpers.R. None is typed by hand. The evidence blocks under each retrospective section are rendered from citations.csv, so the cited titles come from the database rather than from a writer’s memory. ev() fails loudly if a cited id is not in the corpus, which makes a fabricated citation impossible to ship silently.

The census, reconciled

This snapshot is pinned. The tree is Dropbox-synced and the corpus grew from 385 to over 650 resources during a single work session. A document that claims “every figure recomputed, none asserted” cannot have its figures move with a sync, so build_metrics.R is frozen to a fixed id manifest (scenario/data/snapshot_ids.txt). The mapping runs 385 indexed resources → 2 exact url_norm duplicates folded (coarse.ink twice, a Sant’Anna page under two titles) → title-dedup → 375 distinct works, of which 287 carry extracted full text. An earlier audit of this same corpus, run days before, reported a smaller census (294) because the corpus was smaller then. That number is superseded, not contradicted. The AEA-2026 webcast page hosts two distinct talks (Li on the labour market, Ash on language models) sharing one URL. Those are kept as two works, not folded, because a shared landing page is not a shared work.

The flagship number, audited

The essay’s most quotable statistic, how much of the AI-research tooling is verification, was first produced by a keyword classifier (review|referee|replicat|reproduc|citation|audit|verif|integrity, frozen as VERIF_RE). Held to the discipline the essay preaches, that classifier fails an audit. Hand-labelled against a frozen gold set (scenario/data/tool_labels.csv, one row per tool with a verdict and a one-word kind), the audited numbers appear below.

Measure Verification share n / tools
Keyword classifier (VERIF_RE) 65.8% ~25 / 38
Audited gold labels 28.9% 11 / 38

Against the gold labels the keyword classifier has precision 36% and recall 81.8%. The 16 false positives are literature-search aids (Elicit, Scholara, Google Scholar Labs), a LaTeX workspace, and even academic-humanizer, a tool whose purpose is to strip the tells verifiers look for. The 2 false negatives are an adversarial-research loop and a replication-scoring agent it misses. The audited share, 28.9%, is what the prose and prediction P1 now use. That the essay had to correct a number it asserted is not an embarrassment to hide. It is the thesis running on the document itself, because an unaudited filter is just more cheap intelligence. The gold labels are a small public dataset shipped with the site. Adjust them and the number moves, in the open.

The whale is mislabelled

The single largest document, 33.4% of all extracted characters, is indexed as “National Artificial Intelligence Initiative Act.” It is not that Act. Its bytes are House Report 116-617, the full committee report accompanying the FY2021 National Defense Authorization Act, of which the AI Initiative Act is one division. So a third of the corpus’s raw text is a mostly off-topic legislative document under a too-flattering title. The word headline is reported three ways. It is 3,671,256 uncapped, 2,446,068 with the whale removed, and 2,057,167 with the whale and the 388,901-word course-playlist transcript removed. Every per-document text statistic on this site caps document weight at the 95th percentile (172,284 characters) so the whale cannot drive an average.

The four hygiene rules

1. The version graph. The same work shows up as a working paper, a journal version, a video, and a thread. resources_clean.csv dedupes by normalized title before counting works, which folds 10 duplicate versions and leaves 375 works from 385 rows. Coverage keeps all rows, because coverage measures the index, not the field.

2. The whale. One document, a piece of AI legislation, holds 33.4% of all extracted characters. Any text-volume statistic caps per-document weight at the 95th percentile of character counts (172,284 characters). The headline word count (3,671,256) is kept uncapped to match the project’s measured claims passport, while every per-document average uses the capped figure (1,977,820). An uncapped average measures the whale.

3. Canonical names. Author names are diacritic-stripped and case-folded before distinct people are counted, so “Bäckman” and “Backman” are one person.

4. Dates. Roughly half the corpus (199 of 385 rows, 52%) carries no usable date. Undated rows are excluded from every time series and reported as a visible residual on the Timeline page, never silently dropped. The database is not version-controlled (it is rebuilt wholesale each run), so there is no git history to recover approximate dates from. The undated rows stay undated and stay counted.

The metrics strip, and its forecast band

The strip is measured through 2026 H1. After that, panels 2-4 draw an illustrative forecast band, a widening envelope from the last measured point to a range at 2028 H2. The bands are not themselves scored predictions. They are a visual aid, and their endpoints are set by the direction of the numbered predictions:

  • Tool share trends down (capabilities become product defaults), the direction of P9.
  • Verification share of tools trends up (the referee factory keeps growing), P1, P11.
  • Coverage recovers (embargoes lapse), P10.

Panel 1 (cumulative works by category) shows no projection at all past the measured data, because projecting a per-category stack would be a guess dressed as a measurement.

What this document cannot verify

  • Curation is not coverage of the field. A curated corpus measures the curator’s map as much as the territory. The 65.2% of dated works landing in 2026 H1 is partly the field flooding and partly the newest material being the easiest to find. Where the distinction changes a number, the retrospective says so.
  • The verification-tool count is a keyword heuristic. A tool counts as verification tooling when its title or description matches review|referee|replicat|reproduc|citation|audit|verif|integrity. The classifier is used only to bucket a row and emit a count, no description text is published, and it is a legible heuristic, not a taxonomy. It will catch a literature-review tool that is verification-adjacent and miss one described in other words.
  • The narrative substance is not in the database. The story beats, which papers say what, are drawn from a separate analysis (docs/ai_in_economics_discussion_notes.md) and grounded in corpus rows by title. The database supplies the counts and dates. It does not adjudicate the interpretation.
  • The forecast is extrapolation. Nothing past 2028 is measured, and the claims there depend on institutional choices rather than model capability. That is stated at the branch, and the error bars are meant to be read as wide.

Publishability

This site is designed to be publishable, so it obeys the same line the corpus’s own report obeys: aggregates and bibliographic facts only, counts, titles, authors, dates, tags. It never publishes the curator’s descriptions or fetched body text. Titles appear because they are bibliographic facts. The underlying documents do not.

Rebuilding

# refresh the numbers from the database, then render the site
Rscript scenario/R/build_metrics.R      # SQLite -> scenario/data/derived/*.csv
quarto render scenario                   # CSVs -> _site/

The CSVs are committed, so the site renders in continuous integration without the database (the database is gitignored). Refreshing the numbers needs the machine that holds the corpus. Rendering does not.

Every figure on this site is recomputed from data/aiecon.sqlite at build time, and none is asserted. If the prose and the database disagree, the database is right.

Indexing policy

What gets into the corpus, stated once so the “curator’s map” caveat has teeth. In: any resource on AI and economics worth a record, papers, working papers, guides, tools, courses, talks, threads, from any source, added through the seed feed or the hand-curated overlay. Full text in wherever it can be legitimately obtained. Where it cannot, the gap is recorded row by row rather than hidden. Out: nothing on topic is excluded for being informal or un-refereed, a Substack guide and an NBER working paper are both records, but a resource that is a door (a landing page pointing at content it does not itself contain) is marked as such and licenses no claim about what lies behind it. Changelog: the corpus is append-only. The frozen snapshot for this release is scenario/data/snapshot_ids.txt, and later releases re-pin deliberately, never by accident.

Colophon

This document practises the four rules it describes. Machine assistance: the prose was drafted and revised with AI coding agents. Every past claim resolves to a corpus row through ev(), which fails the build on a fabricated id, and every number is recomputed by build_metrics.R rather than typed. Verified how: the tool classification was hand-labelled against a frozen gold set and its keyword classifier audited against it (above). The reconciliation, dedup, and whale relabelling were checked against the database. Where the gold sets live: scenario/data/tool_labels.csv and the snapshot manifest ship with the repository, so a reader can recompute the headline numbers. Frozen at: this release pins to the snapshot manifest. The tagged commit hash belongs here, and the forecast prose freezes at the tag while the retrospective and the predictions scoreboard keep updating. Not yet done, and owned by the curator: an external adjudication panel for the scoreboard, a browsable copy of the database, and publishing the gold set as a standalone dataset.
