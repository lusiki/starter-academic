The read state of the corpus

What has full text, what is embargoed, what is gone

Supplement
Author

Luka Šikić

Published

July 23, 2026

This page recomputes the corpus’s read state from the database. Every figure below is generated at render time from the CSVs in scenario/data/derived/. Unfold any chunk to see the code that made it.

Overall, 74.1% of the 661 indexed resources have extracted full text. That single number hides the story, which is that the gray literature is open and the archival literature is gated. This is the access inversion. Gray categories reach 82.6% full text, while research papers sit at 58%, held down by an embargoed frontier the field is indexing faster than the embargoes lapse.

The embargo queue and the dead

Coverage is not a measure of effort. It is a measure of what is legitimately gettable today. The manual lane holds records with a real document that cannot yet be fetched, and the largest block is NBER working papers under an ~18-month embargo, which age out on their own and are not hand-work. A handful of rows are permanently gone.

The manual / nber_embargo block is the single largest reason archival coverage lags. It is real research with a real document to get, so it belongs in the denominator, but the embargo means it cannot be fetched yet. That is why indexing the frontier honestly lowers the coverage percentage, and why the percentage rises again on its own as embargoes lapse. Prediction P10 scores exactly this recovery.

A resource with lane = no_fulltext is a door, not a defect. It is a page that points at content and is none itself. It licenses no claim about what lies behind it, and it is counted here as what it is.
