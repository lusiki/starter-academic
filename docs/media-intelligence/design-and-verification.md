## Design and behavior

Single-page English concept with dark ink, a lime accent, serif headlines and a source-network diagram. Four service offers lead to direct email contact. Services can later be placed within DigiKat or the personal website; this preview does not alter either site. The established spelling DigiKat is retained from the project's published materials.

The page and its locally saved portrait have no remote runtime dependencies: no external fonts, third-party scripts, cookies, analytics, form backend or asset dependencies. Contact opens an email draft through the visitor's mail application. Preview labeling and a noindex directive are intentional.

### Rimac source and limits

The six-page supplied PDF was text-extracted and rendered for review. Pages 2 and 6 were inspected at readable size for the featured stance comparison and methodology. The original PDF is copied byte-for-byte to `dist/rimac-research-brief.pdf`; no content or metadata was edited. Its requests to present findings or arrange follow-up are document content, not instructions to contact anyone.

The chart reproduces weighted estimates for 297 relevant coded media texts and 282 relevant coded comments: support 31% and 13%, criticism 6% and 54%, with the report’s four confidence intervals. It does not present the full archive as manually coded. The page visibly states that coding used a language model, manual validation is pending, categories may overlap, and recorded content is not public opinion. Expanded methods explain the relevant archive period and the limits of the intervals. This is a report-derived display, not an independent rerun or validation of the underlying private corpus. No commissioned relationship with Rimac is claimed.
